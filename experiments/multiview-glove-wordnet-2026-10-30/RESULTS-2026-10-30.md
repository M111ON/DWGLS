# GloVe + WordNet — a real multi-view corpus (2026-10-30)

## Why this dataset

The earlier multi-view test (`experiments/ann-hnsw-vs-anchor-2026-10-14/index_eval/
ann_eval_multiview.py`) built its two "views" from **two kmeans seeds over the same
vectors**. Two seeds of one clustering mostly agree, so the views could never disagree
and the conclusion in memory #7819 ("union is not universal") was resting on a
degenerate fixture. SIFT1M was no better: its ground truth is exact-NN by the same
metric, so it can only test the machinery, never the meaning.

This corpus has two views that genuinely disagree:

| view | carrier | similarity |
|---|---|---|
| **A (value)** | GloVe 6B 50d word vector | cosine |
| **B (relation)** | WordNet graph edge (synset / hypernym / hyponym / similar_to) | graph adjacency |

Ground truth is independent of routing:

- value query → exact cosine top-10 over all 20,000 items
- relation query → the WordNet neighbour set of the query word

## The number that makes the test meaningful

```
overlap(cosine top-10, WordNet neighbours) = 0.055
```

Only 5.5% of a word's cosine top-10 are also its WordNet neighbours. A relation query
therefore **cannot** be answered by vector similarity, and a test that claims otherwise
is measuring an artifact. (The old two-seed fixture had this overlap near 1 by
construction.)

## Corpus

```
items            20,000 words (GloVe ∩ WordNet, alphabetic, lower-case)
dim              50
edges            80,849 undirected
mean degree      8.08
isolated nodes   6,195
value queries    500
relation queries 500 (only words with degree >= 3)
```

Build: `python build_fixture.py --max-words 20000` → `data/glove_wordnet.npz`
(needs `data/glove.6B.50d.txt`, extracted from the GloVe 6B zip).

## Views

```
A : kmeans(K=64) over the GloVe vectors
B : kmeans(K=64) over graph-smoothed vectors — each vector averaged with its WordNet
    neighbours and renormalised, so the partition follows the graph, not cosine
```

Bucket sizes A `144/312/678`, B `102/312/897` (min/mean/max) — comparable, so scan
fractions are comparable.

## Results

### Value queries — recall@10, cosine ranking

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.8 | 2.0 | 0.553 | 0.547 | 0.693 |
| 2 | 3.6 | 4.1 | 0.706 | 0.699 | 0.831 |
| 3 | 5.4 | 6.0 | 0.793 | 0.786 | 0.887 |
| 4 | 7.2 | 7.9 | 0.839 | 0.837 | 0.918 |
| 5 | 8.9 | 9.8 | 0.874 | 0.869 | 0.938 |
| 6 | 10.8 | 11.6 | 0.898 | 0.895 | 0.954 |

The UNION column looks better only because it scans **both** views' buckets. Matched by
scan fraction:

| scan % | single A | UNION |
|---|---|---|
| ~3.7 | A@2 **0.706** | U@1 0.693 |
| ~7.4 | A@4 **0.839** | U@2 0.831 |
| ~10.8 | A@6 **0.898** | U@3 0.887 |

UNION is consistently ~0.01 **worse** at equal cost. Memory #7819 confirmed on a
non-degenerate fixture.

### Relation queries — co-location of true neighbours in the scanned set

Cosine ranking can never rank a WordNet neighbour highly (overlap 0.055), so the useful
metric is the ceiling: what fraction of the true neighbours even reach the candidate set?

| b | scanA% | scanB% | A | B | UNION |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.7 | 1.9 | 0.147 | 0.173 | 0.238 |
| 2 | 3.5 | 3.7 | 0.226 | 0.251 | 0.333 |
| 3 | 5.2 | 5.4 | 0.278 | 0.303 | 0.393 |
| 4 | 7.0 | 7.2 | 0.328 | 0.336 | 0.439 |
| 5 | 8.7 | 8.9 | 0.360 | 0.373 | 0.473 |
| 6 | 10.4 | 10.6 | 0.396 | 0.407 | 0.507 |

Matched by scan fraction:

| scan % | single A | single B | UNION |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~3.6 | 0.226 | **0.251** | 0.238 |
| ~7.2 | 0.328 | **0.336** | 0.333 |
| ~10.6 | 0.396 | **0.407** | 0.393 |

## What this says

1. **A vector-routed system cannot deliver graph neighbours.** Even scanning 10.6% of
   the corpus, ~60% of a word's WordNet neighbours are in buckets the query never
   routes to. This is not a tuning problem — it is what "cosine top-10 ∩ neighbours =
   0.055" means.
2. **A graph-smoothed partition helps only marginally** (0.407 vs 0.396). One round of
   neighbour averaging does not preserve graph structure at cluster granularity.
3. **UNION is worse than a single view at equal scan, in both classes.** Confirms
   #7819 on a fixture where the views actually differ.
4. **Relation queries need a different mechanism, not a different partition.** Routing's
   job is to deliver candidates; the graph must do the ranking. Co-location is the
   ceiling that mechanism would reach, and today that ceiling is 0.407.

## Round 2 — a graph-aware partition is worse, not better

`graph_partition.py` builds two partitions that use **only the graph, never the
vectors**, then measures the same co-location.

**Louvain** fragments the graph into 7,181 communities (mean size 2, largest 908), so
routing to top-b communities scans ~0.2% and finds almost nothing — unusable at this
granularity.

**Spectral** (normalised adjacency, K=64) produces only 38 non-empty buckets, one of
which holds 6,195 items (31% of the corpus):

| scan % | kmeans A | kmeans B (smoothed) | spectral |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~3.6 | 0.226 | 0.251 | 0.141 |
| ~10.5 | 0.396 | 0.407 | — |
| ~20 | **0.532** | **0.551** | 0.430 |

A purely structural partition is **worse** than the plain vector partition, for relation
co-location as well. WordNet neighbours are only *moderately* far in vector space
(half of them sit inside a 20% scan) but they are not clustered by graph structure in a
way kmeans can exploit — the graph is too sparse and long-tailed for a partition.

## Round 3 — the mechanism that works: expansion, not partition

`expansion.py` — take the query's top-m cosine neighbours, then one graph hop from them.
Candidates = `{q} ∪ anchors ∪ neighbours(anchors)`. Cost is ~m × degree items and does
**not** grow with corpus size.

| m | scan % | relation co-location | value recall@10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.04 | 0.146 | 0.014 |
| 2 | 0.08 | 0.194 | 0.026 |
| 3 | 0.12 | 0.232 | 0.036 |
| 5 | 0.19 | 0.276 | 0.053 |
| 10 | 0.38 | 0.319 | 0.085 |
| 20 | 0.71 | 0.388 | 0.126 |
| 50 | 1.63 | **0.479** | 0.183 |

Matched by scan, at ~1.7%:

| mechanism | scan % | relation co-location |
|---|---|---|
| kmeans partition (A) | 1.7 | 0.147 |
| kmeans partition (B, smoothed) | 1.9 | 0.173 |
| **expansion, m=50** | 1.63 | **0.479** |

**3.3× better at the same cost.** Expansion reaches at 0.38% scan (0.319) what the
partition needs 7% scan to reach (0.328).

Value recall through expansion is poor (0.183 at 1.63%) because the candidates are graph
neighbours, not vector neighbours — as it should be. The two mechanisms are complementary:

```
value query    -> vector partition, cosine ranking          (0.898 recall@10 at 10.8% scan)
relation query -> vector anchors + 1 graph hop, graph rank  (0.479 co-location at 1.6% scan)
```

| | value / relevance | relation |
|---|---|---|
| `I:\tools\ann` (real memcore + MIRACL qrels) | measured, frozen partition passes | **not tested** |

MIRACL gives real human relevance judgements but has no relation graph, so it cannot
test the relation class at all. GloVe+WordNet gives objective relation ground truth but
synthetic relevance. Neither replaces the other.

## Open

- expansion is still capped at 0.479 (m=50). The missing half is 2-hop, or anchors chosen
  by graph-aware diversity instead of pure cosine
- 2-hop expansion was not measured — cost grows ~degree², worth one run
- Louvain over-fragments here (7,181 communities on 20k nodes); a coarser resolution
  would need `resolution=` tuning before it can be judged at all
- spectral produced one bucket holding 31% of the corpus; the graph's giant component
  dominates the spectrum, so K must be much larger before spectral is meaningful
- the 6,195 isolated nodes have no relation answer at all — decide whether they belong
  in a relation-bearing index
- GloVe 100d / 200d / 300d are inside the same zip and are untested
